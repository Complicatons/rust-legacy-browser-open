# Salvaged Signal

**A warning sign that has outlived its warning.**

The mark is a found object: a riveted steel plate with a symbol everyone already knows, so its shape is legible before it is understood. Geometry is exact (true 60° blades, concentric rings, twelve evenly spaced rivets), and time has worked against that exactness. The tension between the drafted form and the weathered surface carries the whole piece.

Color is limited to oxide orange, black enamel and bare rust. The plate is lit from one corner. The outer lip catches light at the top left and falls into shadow at the bottom right, so the disc reads as pressed metal rather than a flat fill. Wear is not random sprinkling. Paint chips gather where hands and weather reach first, at the rim, and only a few flecks break through the enamel. Every value is placed with painstaking care, and nothing is louder than the symbol.

Scale is designed for the smallest view first. At 32 pixels it has to be three blades and a hub, and nothing else. At full size the surface rewards a close look with rivet glints, a rolled rim groove and hairline scratches, but none of that detail may compete with the silhouette. The work should look like hours of patient, master-level finishing went into something that looks abandoned.

There is no text. The symbol speaks.
